What it is

Plainly, and without the part where we oversell it.

Nourishology is a leave-on moisturizing cream. It's built around the things that hold moisture in skin and keep the surface comfortable: ceramides, squalane, glycerin and shea butter, in a base with no fragrance and no drying alcohol.

It was made because someone who spends her working life around irritated skin couldn't find a daily moisturizer she was happy to reach for. That's the whole origin. There isn't a bigger story than that.

What it does

  • Moisturizes, and keeps moisturizing — the barrier ingredients are there so it doesn't wear off in an hour
  • Softens rough, tight, flaky skin
  • Feels comfortable on skin that reacts to most things
  • Absorbs without a film, so it works under sunscreen or makeup

What it is not

  • Not a drug and not a treatment for any condition
  • Not a substitute for seeing a dermatologist or a doctor
  • Not tested in a clinical trial — it's a well-built cosmetic, not a studied medicine
  • Not going to fix something that needs actual medical care

How it works

Three jobs, no mystery.

It replaces what's missing

Skin that feels tight and rough is usually short on lipids and water. Ceramides, squalane and shea butter are the same kinds of materials skin already uses to hold itself together.

It holds water in place

Glycerin and the xylitol sugars draw water in. Ectoin and the ceramide blend help keep it there rather than letting it evaporate through the surface.

It stays out of the way

No fragrance, no essential oils, no drying alcohol — the three things most likely to bother reactive skin. What's left is a cream that's boring in the best sense.

Before you use it

The things worth saying out loud.

Patch test first

Put a small amount on the inside of your forearm and leave it 24 hours before using it more widely. This matters more if your skin reacts to things, and it's worth doing even with a fragrance-free product.

Stop using it if you get redness, burning, itching or a rash, and talk to a doctor if it doesn't settle.

Not for broken skin, and not medical advice

Don't apply it to open, weeping, blistered or infected skin. Nothing here is medical advice, and if something on your skin is painful, spreading, or not getting better, that's a conversation for a doctor rather than a moisturizer.

If you're pregnant, nursing, treating a diagnosed skin condition, or using prescription topicals, check with your clinician before adding anything new.

Keep it clean

This is a water-based cream, which means it can grow bacteria if it's mishandled. Use clean dry hands or a spatula, keep the lid closed, store it out of direct sun, and don't add water to it. If it changes colour, smell or texture, throw it out.
